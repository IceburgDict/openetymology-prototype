03Etymology

The word ‘salary’ has a surprisingly tangible origin, directly linked to the vital commodity of salt. It derives from the Latin ‘salarium’, which was an allowance paid to Roman soldiers specifically for the purchase of salt (‘sal’). Salt was a crucial and valuable substance for preserving food and was sometimes even used as a form of currency. Over time, ‘salarium’ evolved from meaning this specific salt allowance to denote any regular stipend or fixed payment for services. Thus, the modern abstract concept of a regular wage retains the historical echo of a payment in salt, illustrating how fundamental economic practices are embedded in language.
